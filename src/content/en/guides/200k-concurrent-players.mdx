Fishing games are supposed to be quiet. How to Fish ignored that assignment entirely — the physics-based co-op sim from Dazed Games detonated on Steam within days of launch, riding a wave of clip-sharing, co-op chaos, and a review score most studios would frame on a wall.

## The Numbers

- **268,101 all-time peak concurrent players** on Steam
- Roughly **12,260 user reviews** within the first week
- **91.67% positive rating** — a "Very Positive" band on SteamDB
- Launch discount of **38%** through August 27, 2026, which fed the early momentum

For context, those numbers put a self-published fishing simulator in the same launch-week conversation as the year's biggest multiplayer releases.

## Why It Took Off

The game's loop is simple to stream and fun to ruin for your friends. Four players share a boat, a budget, and a physics engine that treats every fish, grenade, and trick shot as a physics problem:

- **Fish, kill, sell** — every catch is loot, and how you finish a kill changes its sell price. Hip-fire finishes pay better than scoped ones, which turns every fight into a highlight reel.
- **Heavy weapons are on the menu.** The community latched onto "physically educating" fish with assault rifles and dynamite, which is exactly as dignified as it sounds.
- **Trick shots and gambling** pad the loop — bonus money for style, roulette for the brave.
- **The premise sells itself:** you get drunk on a boat, crash onto an island, and have to fish your way home.

## What Reviewers Praise — and What They Don't

The 91%+ positive score comes with a consistent pattern in the reviews:

**Praised:** the co-op mayhem, the physics comedy, the pace of progression across the five islands, and the price-to-chaos ratio.

**Criticized:** the usual co-op friction — lobby and connection quirks that show up when four players try to share one boat. ## Is It Still Worth Playing?

Yes — the concurrency spike has cooled from its peak, as all launches do, but the game's core loop does not depend on a live service. Everything in the campaign — five islands, ten bosses, the rare-fish hunt, and the speedboat ending — is playable solo or with friends at any time.
